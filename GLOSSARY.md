# Lexicon of Technical Denim Terminology

The academic and industrial analysis of denim necessitates a highly specific technical vocabulary. The following is an established lexicon of foundational terminology.

## Textile Anatomy and Composition
- **Warp:** The longitudinal (vertical) yarns held in tension on a loom. In traditional denim manufacturing, the warp yarns are rope-dyed with indigo.
- **Weft:** The transverse (horizontal) yarns woven over and under the warp. In denim, the weft typically remains un-dyed (ecru or white), resulting in the characteristic blue exterior and white interior of the textile.
- **Slub:** A morphological irregularity wherein a yarn fluctuates in diameter. When woven, slub yarns produce a highly textured, uneven textile surface.
- **Nep:** Minute knots or fractured fibers that protrude from the textile's surface, visually manifesting as slight white textural anomalies.
- **Loomstate (Unsanforized):** Textile output in its raw, unaltered condition immediately following the weaving process. It has not been subjected to mechanical or chemical stabilization and will exhibit volumetric shrinkage of up to 10% upon initial aqueous saturation.
- **Sanforized:** A mechanical stabilization process (patented by Sanford Cluett in 1930) that pre-shrinks the fabric utilizing a combination of steam, thermal cylinders, and compressive rubber rollers. Sanforized textiles are structurally stable and exhibit minimal shrinkage (1-3%).

## Degradation Morphology (Fading/Atari)
- **Atari:** The Japanese nomenclature for the localized, selective fading of structural ridges and creases on a denim garment resulting from mechanical friction.
- **Whiskers (Hige):** The horizontal and radiating fade vectors that develop across the anterior crotch and upper thighs, generated by the mechanical compression of sitting.
- **Honeycombs (Hachi-no-su):** The complex, hexagonal fade matrices that develop on the posterior knee, resulting from the articulation of the leg.
- **Stacks:** The accordion-like folds that manifest at the distal hem when a garment's inseam significantly exceeds the wearer's physical leg length. These folds generate concentrated horizontal fading patterns.
- **Roping:** The diagonal, helical fading pattern that manifests along the circumference of the hem. It is primarily a structural artifact generated by the mechanical feed anomalies of vintage cylinder-bed chainstitch machines (e.g., the Union Special 43200G).
