## 1. Fabric and Weaving Fundamentals

- **Warp:** The vertical yarns that are held tightly under tension on the loom during weaving. In classic denim, the warp yarns are the ones dyed with beautiful blue indigo.
- **Weft:** The horizontal yarns that are woven over and under the warp. In most denim, the weft is left undyed (white or natural ecru), which is why your jeans are blue on the outside and white on the inside!
- **Slub:** A cool, intentional irregularity where the yarn gets thicker and thinner in spots. When woven together, slubby yarns give the denim a very textured, bumpy, and unique character.
- **Nep:** Tiny knots or broken fibers that stick out from the surface of the fabric. They look like little white flecks or snowy textures on the denim.
- **Loomstate (Unsanforized):** Denim in its most natural, raw form right off the loom. It hasn't been treated with heat or chemicals to prevent shrinking, so it will shrink quite a bit (up to 10%) the first time it hits water.
- **Sanforized:** A process invented in 1930 by Sanford L. Cluett that pre-shrinks the fabric at the mill using steam, heat, and rubber rollers. Sanforized jeans are ready to wear and will only shrink a tiny bit (about 1-3%) when washed.
- **Ring-Spun Cotton:** The traditional spinning method where cotton fibers are continuously twisted and thinned onto a bobbin. It creates strong, dense yarn with natural micro-variations that yield vintage-style texture and character.
- **Open-End Spinning:** A high-speed modern spinning method introduced in the late 1960s where air turbines form yarn without a spindle. It produces a flatter, more uniform yarn with less slub and softer abrasion contrast.
- **Right Hand Twill (RHT):** The most common denim twill weave, running diagonally upwards from bottom-left to top-right. Pioneered by Levi's, RHT packs yarns tightly, yielding crisp, sharp fades.
- **Left Hand Twill (LHT):** Championed by Lee, the diagonal weave line runs upwards from bottom-right to top-left. Because the weave direction aligns with the twist of S-twist yarns, it feels noticeably softer to the touch and produces fuzzier, more blended fades.
- **Broken Twill:** Invented by Wrangler in 1964 to combat the natural tendency of twill jeans to twist around the wearer's leg after washing. The diagonal weave alternates direction in a zig-zag pattern, balancing fabric tension and keeping the outseam straight.
- **Selvedge (Self-Edge):** The tightly woven, clean border of cloth produced on traditional narrow shuttle looms (about 30 inches wide). The shuttle carries the weft yarn back and forth in a continuous loop, preventing edges from unraveling.
- **Selvedge ID:** The colored ticker thread running down the selvedge line (classic red-line for Cone Mills/Levi's, plain all-white for 19th-century Amoskeag, green, blue, or yellow for specialty mills).

## 2. Dyeing, Chemistry, and Color

- **Ring Dyeing:** The hallmark of true denim warp yarn. Because indigo molecules are large and oxidize quickly, they do not penetrate all the way through to the cotton fiber core. The outside is stained deep indigo while the center remains pure white. As surface dye rubs away, that white core is exposed, creating the iconic fade.
- **Leuco-Indigo:** The chemically reduced, water-soluble form of indigo found inside dye vats. It is actually a pale yellow-green color!
- **Skying:** The process of hoisting freshly dipped yarn high into towering vertical air ranges ("skying ranges"). Atmospheric oxygen instantly oxidizes the yellow-green leuco-indigo into insoluble, deep blue pigment.
- **Sky Tender:** Historical mill operator who monitored yarn tension and oxygen exposure along the elevated skying catwalks in the dye house.
- **Sukumo:** The traditional Japanese composted indigo dye made from *Polygonum tinctorium* (tadeai) leaves, fermented and tended for 120 days in the *nedoko* ("bedroom").
- **Jigoku-Date:** Literally translated as "producing hell," an ancient Tokushima vat recipe that ferments sukumo with hardwood ash lye (*aku*), slaked shell lime (*shikkui*), and wheat bran (*fusuma*) without synthetic chemicals.
- **Woad (*Isatis tinctoria*):** A flowering European biennial plant cultivated since the Middle Ages (famous in Toulouse and Languedoc) yielding a soft, historic pastel-blue indigo dye.
- **Cast / Tint:** The subtle undertone of the denim. A "green cast" comes from sulfur bottoming or natural vegetable indigo (common in late 1800s Amoskeag denim); a "red cast" leans toward purplish royal blue; a "grey cast" has a cool, ash-tinted wash.

## 3. Garment Anatomy and Hardware

- **Arcuate:** The signature double-arched decorative stitching on back pockets, famously patented by Levi Strauss & Co. in 1873.
- **Lazy S:** The wavy horizontal stitching found on Lee back pockets, officially registered in 1944.
- **Cinch / Buckle-Back:** An adjustable fabric strap with a metal buckle located on the rear waistband or yoke of 19th and early 20th-century work pants, used to cinch in the waist before belt loops became standard.
- **Hidden Rivets:** Copper pocket rivets concealed under the folded top edge of the back pocket lining (introduced by Levi's in 1937) so the metal burrs wouldn't scratch up wooden furniture, leather saddles, or car upholstery.
- **Red Tab:** The tiny folded cloth ribbon introduced in 1936 by Levi's sales manager Chris Lucier on the right back pocket to make Levi's identifiable from a distance.
- **Big E vs. small e:** Refers to the capitalisation of Levi's Red Tabs. Garments made before 1971 feature "LEVI'S" in all-capital letters ("Big E") and command top-tier collector status; post-1971 tabs read "Levi's" with a lowercase 'e'.
- **Yoke:** The V-shaped horizontal seam piece sitting between the waistband and the seat of the jeans. It provides three-dimensional shaping to hug the hips without needing darts.
- **Coin / Watch Pocket:** The small fifth pocket tucked inside the front right pocket, originally designed in the 1870s to hold a pocket watch on a chain.
- **Bar Tack:** A tight, dense cluster of zig-zag reinforcement stitches (often sewn on industrial machines like the Juki LK-1854) applied to pocket corners, fly bases, and belt loops in place of metal rivets.
- **Laurel Wreath Button:** A hollow metal tack button stamped with a laurel leaf ring, standardized during WWII material rationing (1942–1945) when the US War Production Board restricted proprietary branded hardware.
- **Keyhole Buttonhole:** A specialized buttonhole featuring a rounded circular hole at the front edge (sewn by machines like the Reece 101), providing clearance for a thick metal tack button shank so the waistband sits flat.
- **Hair-on-Hide:** Lee’s iconic vintage back patch made from un-shorn cowhide with the natural cattle hair preserved, branded with hot irons.
- **Pliers Pocket:** A narrow vertical tool slot on the right rear thigh of early 1880s waist overalls and utility carpenter pants.

## 4. Jackets and Heritage Silhouettes

- **Type I Jacket:** Levi's iconic 1905–1953 blouse (Lot 506XX). Recognizable by its single left chest pocket, dual front vertical pleats, boxy cut, and rear cinch-buckle.
- **Type II Jacket:** Introduced in 1953 (Lot 507XX). Features two chest pockets, front pleats, and button-tab waist adjusters instead of the rear cinch.
- **Type III ("Trucker") Jacket:** Introduced in 1962 (Lot 557XX / 70505). Characterized by pointed flap chest pockets, angled tapering "V" front seams, and no pleats. The blueprint for modern denim jackets worldwide.
- **Union-All:** A one-piece work coverall combining a work shirt/jacket with dungarees into a single suit, invented by Henry David Lee in 1913 in Salina, Kansas.
- **Dungarees:** Coarse work trousers or overalls, named after *dungrı́*, a rugged Indian cotton cloth exported through the port of Dongari (Mumbai) since the 17th century.

## 5. The Art of Fading and Wear (Atari)

- **Atari:** The Japanese word for "fade." It refers to the beautiful way the indigo dye rubs off the raised edges and creases of your jeans over time.
- **Whiskers (Hige):** The horizontal fade lines that fan out across the lap and upper thighs, created naturally just by sitting down and bending your legs.
- **Honeycombs (Hachi-no-su):** The awesome, web-like geometric fade patterns that develop behind the knees as the fabric bunches up when you walk and bend.
- **Stacks:** The bunched-up folds of fabric that settle around your ankles when your jeans are longer than your legs. Over time, these folds create striking horizontal fade lines.
- **Roping:** A fading pattern that looks like a diagonal, twisted rope along the very bottom hem of the jeans. This highly sought-after detail is actually a beautiful imperfection caused by how vintage chainstitch machines (like the legendary Union Special 43200G) feed the fabric!
- **Train Tracks:** Parallel vertical fade streaks running down the outseam of the pants, formed as the two folded selvedge allowances press against the outer face of the denim over months of wear and pressing.
- **Leg Twist:** The rotation of the outseam toward the front of the ankle on unsanforized twill jeans. It is caused by the natural shrinkage torque of the yarns when washed for the first time.
- **Marbling:** Irregular, blotchy white streaks caused by washing raw denim in a modern washing machine with a high-speed spin cycle, where bunched creases scrape against the drum.

## 6. Vintage and Restoration Terminology

- **Deadstock (NOS):** New Old Stock. Original vintage garments that were manufactured decades ago but remained unsold, unworn, and unwashed in factory packaging or stockroom shelving.
- **Boro:** A traditional Japanese folk textile art born from necessity, where worn garments and futon covers are continuously patched, mended, and layered with indigo scraps over generations.
- **Sashiko:** "Little stabs." Traditional Japanese functional hand-stitching with thick white cotton thread used to reinforce high-wear points, repair tears, and add decorative geometric strength.
- **Darning:** Industrial mending method using a free-motion straight-stitch machine to weave dense threads back and forth across a blowout, rebuilding the structural foundation of frayed denim.
