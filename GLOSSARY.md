# The Denim Glossary

Denim culture has a highly specific vocabulary. Here are the essential terms every maker and enthusiast should know:

## Fabric & Texture
- **Warp:** The vertical yarns on a loom. In denim, the warp is usually dyed indigo.
- **Weft:** The horizontal yarns woven over and under the warp. In denim, the weft is usually left undyed (white), which is why jeans are blue on the outside and white on the inside.
- **Slub:** Yarns that fluctuate in thickness. Woven together, they create a highly textured, uneven fabric surface. 
- **Nep:** Small knots or broken fibers that protrude from the surface of the denim, looking like tiny white snow flurries.
- **Loomstate (Unsanforized):** Denim straight off the loom that has not been treated for shrinkage. It will shrink up to 10% after its first wash.
- **Sanforized:** A mechanical process (invented by Sanford Cluett in 1930) that pre-shrinks the fabric using steam and rubber rollers. Sanforized jeans will only shrink 1-3% when washed.

## Fading (Atari)
- **Atari:** The Japanese term for the selective fading of ridges and creases on a pair of jeans.
- **Whiskers (Hige):** The horizontal fade lines that form around the crotch and upper thighs from sitting.
- **Honeycombs (Hachi-no-su):** The hexagonal fade patterns that form behind the knees.
- **Stacks:** The bunched-up fabric at the ankle when jeans are worn long. Stacking creates accordion-like fade lines.
- **Roping:** The diagonal, rope-like fade that occurs at the hem of the jeans, primarily achieved when hemmed on a vintage Union Special chainstitch machine.
