## 1. The Pre-Soak for Unsanforized and Loomstate Denim

If your new jeans are cut from unsanforized ("loomstate") denim, you must give them an initial soak before wearing, hemming, or altering them!

1. **Tub Preparation:** Fill a bathtub or deep basin with lukewarm-to-hot water.
2. **Submersion:** Turn the jeans inside out and submerge them completely for 45 to 60 minutes. Use a heavy object (like shampoo bottles or clean stones) to keep them from floating.
3. **Drip-Dry:** Remove them gently without twisting or wringing out the fabric. Hang them up by the belt loops or hem in a shower or outside in the shade to air dry.

*Why do this?* An initial soak dissolves the stiff cornstarch and sizing applied during mill slashing and allows the raw cotton fibers to shrink to their permanent dimensions (typically 7% to 10% in length and 4% to 6% in waist). Skipping the pre-soak before hemming guarantees that the pant legs will shrink inches too short after your very first wash!

## 2. Understanding Leg Twist and Twill Torque

When unsanforized jeans are soaked for the first time, you will often notice that the outseams rotate diagonally toward the front of your ankles. This is called **leg twist**.

- **Right Hand Twill (RHT):** The warp yarns are woven diagonally upward to the right. When the fabric absorbs water, the fibers contract along the line of weave tension, causing the outseams to naturally twist clockwise (toward the right ankle).
- **Left Hand Twill (LHT):** Woven diagonally to the left, resulting in counter-clockwise twist (toward the left ankle).
- **Is it a flaw?** Not at all! True vintage enthusiasts celebrate leg twist as the definitive fingerprint of authentic, untreated 1940s and 50s loomstate denim. (If you prefer your outseams to remain perfectly straight, choose jeans woven with **Broken Twill**, invented specifically to neutralize this torque).

## 3. Routine Washing and Maintenance

Letting dirt, grit, skin cells, and sweat accumulate inside the denim weave is the single biggest cause of sudden crotch blowouts. Grit particles act like microscopic razor blades that saw through cotton warp and weft fibers with every step. When your jeans are dirty, wash them!

1. **Turn Inside Out:** Protects the surface indigo from abrasive friction against the washing drum.
2. **Wash in Cold Water:** Cold water minimizes dye loss and prevents excessive secondary shrinking.
3. **Mild, Bleach-Free Detergent:** Use a gentle liquid soap formulated for dark garments or dedicated denim wash (avoid powders containing optical brighteners or bleaching agents).
4. **Preventing Washing Machine "Marbling":** High-speed spin cycles in front-loading washing machines are the enemy of raw denim. When jeans are spun at 1200+ RPM, the stiff wet fabric is crushed against the perforated drum, causing severe localized abrasion that leaves ugly, permanent white lightning streaks ("marbling"). **Rule:** Always select the gentle cycle and turn the spin cycle completely off (or set to 400 RPM max). Remove the dripping jeans promptly.
5. **Always Hang Dry:** Never tumble-dry raw denim. High machine heat shrinks cotton unnaturally, bakes in unwanted creases, and dries out the natural oils in leather back patches, causing them to crack and shrivel.

## 4. Preservation, Repair, and Folk Craft

- **Catch Blowouts Early:** Inspect the crotch, pocket bags, and hem folds regularly. The moment you see the fabric thinning or weft threads beginning to separate, repair it immediately. Small reinforcements prevent catastrophic rips that require massive patch jobs later.
- **Machine Darning:** The modern standard for invisible reinforcement. Using a free-motion straight-stitch machine, a skilled tailor lays down a lightweight cotton backing fabric beneath the fray and weaves thousands of tiny stitches parallel to the warp and weft, rebuilding the lost cotton matrix.
- **Sashiko & Boro (Folk Repair):** Originating in Edo-period Japan, *boro* ("tattered rags") and *sashiko* ("little stabs") celebrate visible mending. Using thick white or indigo-dyed cotton thread, hand-sewn geometric running stitches anchor vintage indigo patches over tears, turning wear holes into unique, handcrafted textile art.

## 5. Busting Common Denim Myths

- **The Freezer Myth:**
  * *The Claim:* Placing raw jeans in a plastic bag in the freezer kills odor-causing bacteria.
  * *The Reality:* Freezing temperatures do not sterilize fabric; they merely place bacteria into dormancy. The instant your body heat warms the denim back to room temperature, the bacteria reactivate and the odor returns. Only surfactant water washes remove bacterial colonies.
- **The Ocean Wash Myth:**
  * *The Claim:* Wading into the ocean and scrubbing jeans with beach sand creates the ultimate natural fade.
  * *The Reality:* Ocean saltwater contains heavy mineral salts and micro-grit that dry out cotton fibers, making them brittle and prone to premature tearing. If you ever participate in an ocean wash ritual, thoroughly rinse the jeans in clean fresh water immediately afterward.
- **The Six-Month Rule Myth:**
  * *The Claim:* You must wear jeans for at least six months without washing to get good fades.
  * *The Reality:* Delaying the first wash does create sharper, high-contrast honeycombs and whiskers because the indigo is abraded dry off the high creases. However, delaying too long without washing weakens the cotton and invites crotch blowouts. If you wear your jeans hard or in warm weather, wash them whenever they are dirty—moderately washed raw denim develops beautiful, vintage-style blue-sky fades without sacrificing fabric lifespan!
