# Textile Preservation and Degradation Protocols

Considerable misinformation surrounds the maintenance protocols for raw denim. This document provides an objective analysis of how to process, clean, and preserve denim garments to maximize structural longevity and achieve high-contrast degradation patterns (fading).

## Initial Saturation Protocols (Unsanforized Textiles Exclusive)
If a garment is constructed from *Loomstate (Unsanforized)* denim, it is imperative to execute a controlled saturation process prior to any mechanical wear or construction.
1. Submerge the garment in a high-temperature aqueous bath.
2. Invert the garment (inside out) and ensure full submersion for 45 to 60 minutes.
3. Extract the garment and allow for ambient air dehydration (hang dry).
*Mechanical Rationale:* High-temperature saturation solubilizes the industrial starches applied during the weaving process and induces the cotton fibers to contract to their final physical dimensions (reducing up to 10% in volume). Bypassing this protocol prior to construction or wear will result in severe seam puckering and a catastrophic loss of fit upon subsequent washing.

## Routine Maintenance Protocols
The accumulation of particulate matter (dirt, grit) and biological secretions (sweat, oils) acts as a microscopic abrasive agent within the cotton fibers, accelerating structural failure (e.g., "crotch blowouts"). When a garment exhibits olfactory degradation or visible soiling, it must be cleansed.
1. **Inversion:** Turn the garment inside out. This shields the exterior indigo dye from the mechanical abrasion of the washing apparatus drum.
2. **Thermal Regulation:** Utilize a cold-water cycle to inhibit excessive indigo discharge and prevent further textile contraction.
3. **Chemical Selection:** Employ a mild, non-bleaching detergent. Harsh chemical surfactants will strip the indigo prematurely.
4. **Dehydration:** Hang dry exclusively. Introducing raw denim to the intense thermal and mechanical stress of a commercial tumble dryer compromises the structural integrity of the leather patch and introduces erratic, unnatural shrinkage vectors.

## Analysis of Common Maintenance Misinformation
- **The "Cryogenic" Fallacy (Freezing Denim):** 
  - *Analysis:* It is a common myth that subjecting denim to sub-zero temperatures within a domestic freezer eradicates odor-causing bacteria. Microbiological analysis confirms that freezing merely induces a state of dormancy in the bacteria; upon returning to ambient physiological temperatures, the bacteria reactivate and olfactory emissions resume. Routine aqueous washing is the only effective sterilization method.
- **Saline Marine Washing (Ocean Washing):**
  - *Analysis:* While popular in enthusiast subcultures, saturating a garment in marine environments exposes the textile to high concentrations of sodium chloride and abrasive silica (sand). These elements aggressively degrade cellulosic fibers. If a marine wash is executed, the garment must be immediately flushed with immense volumes of fresh water to neutralize the saline residue.
- **The Extended Abstinence Protocol (Six-Month Delay):**
  - *Analysis:* Delaying the initial wash cycle for a period of six months *does* empirically produce higher-contrast fading patterns, as the indigo is mechanically abraded from the stress creases before aqueous exposure can uniformly discharge the dye. However, this extended delay allows biological and environmental contaminants to degrade the cotton infrastructure, substantially reducing the garment's operational lifespan.
