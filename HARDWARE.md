# Denim Hardware: Buttons, Rivets & Zippers

Hardware is the structural backbone of denim. Because early workwear endured immense stress, standard sewing threads were not enough to hold pockets together—which led to the invention of the copper rivet.

## 🔨 Rivets & Burrs
Rivets are placed at high-tension areas (pocket corners, the crotch) to prevent the fabric from tearing.
- **Copper Burrs (Washer Rivets):** The traditional, heavy-duty rivet. A copper post is pushed through the fabric, a washer (burr) is placed over it, and the post is hammered or pressed flat. 
- **Hidden Rivets:** Introduced in 1937. Customers complained that exposed copper rivets on the back pockets were scratching their saddles and furniture. Brands began sewing the rivets *under* the denim of the back pockets. 
- **Bar Tacks:** By the 1960s, heavy zig-zag stitching (bar tacks) largely replaced hidden rivets as a cheaper, flatter alternative for back pockets.

## 🔘 Tack Buttons
Unlike standard sewn buttons, denim buttons are punched through the fabric and secured with a metal tack.
- **Donut Buttons:** A button with a hole in the center. Heavily used during WWII (like on the 1944 LVC 501s) to save metal for the war effort.
- **Laurel Wreath Buttons:** Also a WWII staple. Because the US government mandated uniform hardware to save resources, many brands used generic buttons stamped with a laurel wreath (a symbol of peace/victory).

## 🤐 Fly Construction: Buttons vs. Zippers
- **The Button Fly:** The historically accurate, bulletproof choice for raw denim. As denim shrinks and stretches, a button fly conforms to the fabric.
- **The Zip Fly:** Introduced widely in the 1950s (notably by Lee). 
   - *Note on Zippers and Raw Denim:* If you use a zipper on *unsanforized* (shrink-to-fit) denim, the fabric will shrink when washed, but the metal zipper tape will not. This causes an unsightly buckling or "bacon" effect on the crotch. Always use a button fly for unsanforized denim!
   - *Historic Brands:* **Talon** and **Scovill** were the historic American zipper suppliers, though **YKK** (Universal) is the modern standard.
