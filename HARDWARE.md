# Mechanical Fasteners and Structural Hardware

Hardware functions as the structural foundation of a denim garment. Because early utility wear was subjected to immense mechanical stress, standard tensile threads were insufficient to maintain seam integrity. This engineering deficit directly catalyzed the invention of the copper rivet.

## Rivets and Structural Burrs
Rivets are strategically integrated at high-tension intersections (e.g., pocket apices and the crotch juncture) to prevent catastrophic fabric tearing.

- **Copper Burrs (Washer Rivets):** The historically robust rivet configuration. A copper post is mechanically driven through the textile substrate, a secondary washer (the burr) is fitted over the post, and the excess post is hydraulically or manually flattened to secure the joint.
- **Concealed ("Hidden") Rivets:** Engineered in 1937 in response to consumer complaints regarding exposed copper rivets abrading equestrian saddles and domestic furniture. Manufacturers modified the assembly process to install the structural rivets beneath the primary textile layer of the rear pockets.
- **Bar Tacks:** By the 1960s, dense zig-zag stitching (the bar tack) largely superseded concealed rivets as a more economically efficient and geometrically flat alternative for reinforcing rear pockets.

## Tack Buttons
Unlike conventional sewn buttons, denim closures utilize buttons that mechanically puncture the textile and are secured via a rigid metallic tack.

- **"Donut" Buttons:** Characterized by a central void (resembling a torus). This configuration was predominantly utilized during the World War II era (e.g., the 1944 LVC 501) as an austerity measure to conserve metal for military manufacturing.
- **"Laurel Wreath" Buttons:** A secondary wartime artifact. As the United States Government mandated standardized hardware to minimize manufacturing redundancies, numerous brands adopted generic buttons embossed with a laurel wreath (a classical motif representing victory).

## Fly Construction: Fastener Typologies
The selection of the primary crotch fastener fundamentally dictates the garment's functionality and interaction with post-production shrinkage.

- **The Button Fly:** The historically authentic and structurally superior configuration for raw denim. As unsanforized denim shrinks and subsequently stretches during wear, a button fly matrix maintains sufficient mechanical flexibility to conform to the textile's fluctuations.
- **The Zip Fly:** Widely adopted in the 1950s (notably pioneered by the H.D. Lee Mercantile Co.). 
   - *Technical Contraindication regarding Unsanforized Denim:* Implementing a zipper on an *unsanforized* (shrink-to-fit) textile is mechanically flawed. Upon saturation and washing, the cotton substrate will contract by up to 10%, while the metallic zipper tape maintains its original dimensions. This dimensional discrepancy results in severe structural distortion and buckling along the crotch seam. A button fly must be utilized for all unsanforized applications.
   - *Historical Manufacturers:* **Talon** and **Scovill** were the primary historical suppliers of American zippers. In modern manufacturing, **YKK** (and its vintage reproduction subsidiary, Universal) serves as the industry standard, alongside specialty reproduction entities such as **Waldes**.
