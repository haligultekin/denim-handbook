# The History of Denim

Understanding the roots of denim gives us a deeper appreciation for the fabric and construction techniques we use today.

## 🌍 Origins
- **Serge de Nîmes:** The fabric's name is widely believed to originate from Nîmes, France. Weavers there attempted to reproduce a hard-wearing cotton fabric from Genoa, Italy (which gave us the word "Jeans" from the French word for Genoa: *Gênes*).
- **Dungaree:** A historical term referring to a coarse, thick cotton cloth, often dyed blue, originally originating from Dongari Killa in Mumbai, India.

## 👖 The Workwear Era
In the late 19th century, denim became the fabric of choice for American laborers, miners, and cowboys due to its incredible durability. The introduction of copper riveting to reinforce stress points (like pocket corners and the crotch) birthed the modern 5-pocket jean.

## 🧵 The Evolution of Production
- **The Shuttle Loom Era:** Up until the mid-20th century, denim was woven on narrow shuttle looms, creating a finished "selvedge" edge. This slower process created fabric with unique character and natural irregularities.
- **The Modern Era:** As global demand skyrocketed in the 1950s and 60s, the industry shifted to wider, faster projectile looms, sacrificing the self-finished edge for mass-production efficiency. Today, vintage shuttle looms are heavily prized by the community for the heritage fabric they produce.
