# The Anatomy of Thread: What Holds Your Jeans Together

The structural integrity of a denim garment depends heavily on the type of thread used to sew it. The thread you choose doesn't just dictate how long your jeans will last—it also plays a huge role in how they look as they age and fade over time!

## Core vs. Cotton: Choosing Your Material

The biggest decision in thread selection is balancing vintage, historical authenticity against modern durability. 

- **100% Cotton Thread:** This was the industry standard before the 1960s. It yields a beautiful, organic aesthetic and fades naturally right alongside your indigo denim. However, cotton has a lower tensile strength. If you build a pair of jeans entirely with cotton thread, you should expect to do some frequent repairs and restitching as the thread naturally breaks down from daily wear and tear. 
- **Poly-Core Thread:** This is the modern go-to for most makers. Poly-core thread features a super-strong polyester core wrapped in a cotton exterior. This brilliant composite gives you the hardcore strength of polyester while keeping the classic, matte look of cotton on the surface.

## Understanding Tex Sizing (Thread Thickness)

In the sewing world, thread thickness is measured using the "Tex" system. Tex simply refers to the weight in grams of 1,000 meters of that thread. The higher the Tex number, the thicker and more heavy-duty the thread!

- **Tex 40 (Lightweight):** You'll generally only use this for non-structural work, like serging (overlocking) the raw edges of your fabric on the inside of the jeans.
- **Tex 60 - 80 (Mid-weight):** The versatile standard used for general assembly and sewing most of the main seams.
- **Tex 105 - 120 (Heavyweight):** The go-to for those beautiful, highly visible topstitches and structural reinforcement, especially on heavier denim.
- **Tex 135+ (Ultra-Heavy):** Reserved for decorative topstitching on extremely heavy, dense fabrics (like 21oz+ denim). You'll definitely need an industrial machine with heavy-duty needles to handle this beast!

## Matching Thread Color to Your Hardware

Picking the right thread color isn't just about matching the denim; it's about making sure your thread coordinates perfectly with your rivets and buttons for a cohesive look.

- **Warm Hardware Integration:** If you're using classic copper rivets and brass buttons, warm thread colors are your best friend. Historically, shades known as *Jupiter Rust*, *Golden Rod*, or *Tobacco* are used because they blend beautifully with the natural oxidation and patina of copper over time.
- **Cool Hardware Integration:** Working with nickel, silver, or gunmetal hardware? You'll want a cooler thread color profile. Pale yellows, muted lemons, or even stark monochromatic threads look amazing next to the crisp sheen of silvery metals.

## Washing, Bleaching, and Fading

The material your thread is made of will drastically affect what happens if you heavily wash or bleach your jeans later on. 

- **The Polyester Factor:** Polyester doesn't react to standard bleach. If you sew a pair of jeans with poly-core thread and subject them to an intense bleach wash, the indigo denim will lose its color, but the thread will stay exactly the same! This can leave you with a very intense contrast (imagine bright orange thread popping against white, bleached denim). If your goal is for the thread to lose color at the same rate as the fabric, you'll need to stick with 100% cotton thread.
